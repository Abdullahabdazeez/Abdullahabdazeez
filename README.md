## About me

I studied Urban and Regional Planning at the Federal University of Technology, Akure, and gradually moved deeper into GIS, remote sensing and spatial analysis because I wanted to answer planning questions with evidence rather than intuition alone.

Most of my work starts with a practical question: where is access poor, where is environmental pressure increasing, where is urban growth happening, or which places deserve closer planning attention? I then build the analysis around that question and try to keep the final result clear enough for both technical and non-technical readers.

My main interest is **geospatial decision support for sustainable, resilient and equitable urban and environmental planning**.

## Selected work

### Ibadan — land-cover change and urban expansion

<a href="https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ibadan-lulc-change">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ibadan-lulc-change/main/outputs/maps/final_lulc_comparison.svg" alt="Ibadan land-cover change comparison" width="100%">
</a>

I rebuilt and validated a Landsat-based land-cover analysis for Ibadan between 2013 and 2023. Built-up land increased from **99.866 km² to 330.177 km²**, while **246.104 km²** of land mapped as vegetation in 2013 became built-up by 2023. The final locked holdout achieved **87.50% overall accuracy**.

**What it shows:** remote sensing, Random Forest classification, change detection, validation design and planning interpretation.

[View project →](https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ibadan-lulc-change)

### Lagos — access to formal public transport

<a href="https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/lagos-public-transport-accessibility">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Abdullahabdazeez/lagos-public-transport-accessibility/main/assets/maps/01_Lagos_Final_30min_Formal_Transit_Access_Gap.png" alt="Lagos 30-minute formal transit access gap" width="100%">
</a>

I used a pedestrian network, WorldPop population data and **219 mapped formal/core transit access points** to estimate walking access to Lagos's formal public-transport network. Under the reference model, **53.44%** of the analysed population was within 30 minutes of mapped formal transit, while **46.56%** was outside that threshold or affected by a structural network gap.

**What it shows:** network analysis, accessibility modelling, population allocation, sensitivity testing and transport-planning interpretation.

[View project →](https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/lagos-public-transport-accessibility)

### Kano — primary healthcare accessibility

<a href="https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/kano-primary-healthcare-accessibility">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Abdullahabdazeez/kano-primary-healthcare-accessibility/main/assets/project_cover/kano_phc_accessibility_project_cover.png" alt="Kano primary healthcare accessibility project" width="100%">
</a>

This project combines road-network travel time, population demand and a 15-minute two-step floating catchment area analysis across **1,584 PHCs, 484 wards and 16,789 demand cells**. I also tested +5, +10 and +20 PHC expansion scenarios. The +20 scenario improved the statewide population-weighted accessibility score by **1.26%**, showing that new facilities can help selected communities but are not a complete statewide solution on their own.

**What it shows:** healthcare accessibility, 2SFCA, scenario testing, candidate-site evaluation and spatial equity analysis.

[View project →](https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/kano-primary-healthcare-accessibility)

### Ayetoro — coastal vulnerability and adaptation planning

<a href="https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ayetoro-3d-coastal-vulnerability">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ayetoro-3d-coastal-vulnerability/main/assets/maps/01_Ayetoro_Final_Building_Vulnerability.png" alt="Ayetoro coastal vulnerability project" width="100%">
</a>

I assessed coastal vulnerability for a validated inventory of **1,628 buildings** in Ayetoro, Ondo State. The final model combines shoreline proximity, low elevation, building density, road density and facility exposure. **72.17%** of assessed buildings fall within the High or Very High relative vulnerability classes. The 3D component is kept separate from the vulnerability model and is used only for communication and planning visualisation.

**What it shows:** coastal-risk analysis, building-level GIS, model validation, sensitivity analysis and climate-adaptation planning.

[View project →](https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ayetoro-3d-coastal-vulnerability)

## How I approach projects

I try to keep the analysis tied to the decision it is meant to inform. That means checking whether the data really support the conclusion, separating model assumptions from observed evidence, testing results where possible, and stating limitations plainly.

I also treat correction as part of good analysis. When a later review shows that a result is weak, I would rather document the problem and rebuild the workflow than leave a polished but unsupported result in the portfolio.
