## About me

My background is in Urban and Regional Planning. GIS and remote sensing became central to my work because they let me examine planning questions with evidence: where development is spreading, which communities have poor access to services, and where environmental pressure is becoming more serious.

I am particularly interested in **geospatial decision support for sustainable, resilient and equitable urban and environmental planning**. My projects combine spatial analysis with a planning question, and I try to explain the result in language that is useful beyond GIS itself.

## Selected projects

### Ibadan · Land-cover change and urban expansion

<a href="https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ibadan-lulc-change"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ibadan-lulc-change/main/outputs/maps/final_lulc_comparison.svg" alt="Ibadan land-cover comparison" width="100%"></a>

I mapped land-cover change between 2013 and 2023 and rebuilt the validation after an audit showed that the original evidence needed to be stronger. Built-up land increased from **99.866 km² to 330.177 km²**, and **246.104 km²** mapped as vegetation in 2013 became built-up by 2023. The final locked holdout recorded **14 correct cases out of 16**.

**Skills:** Landsat · Random Forest · change detection · validation · planning interpretation  
[View project →](https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ibadan-lulc-change)

### Kano · Primary healthcare accessibility

<a href="https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/kano-primary-healthcare-accessibility"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Abdullahabdazeez/kano-primary-healthcare-accessibility/main/assets/project_cover/kano_phc_accessibility_project_cover.png" alt="Kano primary healthcare accessibility" width="100%"></a>

### Lagos · Access to formal public transport

<a href="https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/lagos-public-transport-accessibility"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Abdullahabdazeez/lagos-public-transport-accessibility/main/assets/maps/01_Lagos_Final_30min_Formal_Transit_Access_Gap.png" alt="Lagos formal transit access gap" width="100%"></a>

Using a pedestrian network, WorldPop data and **219 mapped formal/core transit access points**, I estimated how much of the analysed population could reach formal transit within 30 minutes on foot. The reference model placed **53.44%** within that threshold and **46.56%** outside it or behind a structural network gap.

**Skills:** OSMnx · accessibility modelling · population allocation · transport planning  
[View project →](https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/lagos-public-transport-accessibility)

### Ayetoro · Coastal vulnerability and adaptation planning

<a href="https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ayetoro-3d-coastal-vulnerability"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ayetoro-3d-coastal-vulnerability/main/assets/maps/01_Ayetoro_Final_Building_Vulnerability.png" alt="Ayetoro building vulnerability" width="100%"></a>

I assessed **1,628 buildings** using shoreline proximity, elevation, building density, road density and facility exposure. **72.17%** of the assessed buildings fall in the High or Very High relative-vulnerability classes. The separate 3D component is used for communication rather than as evidence in the vulnerability score.

**Skills:** coastal GIS · building-level analysis · sensitivity testing · adaptation planning  
[View project →](https://github.com/Abdullahabdazeez/ayetoro-3d-coastal-vulnerability)

## How I work

I start with the planning question, then choose the data and method around it. I check assumptions, separate model output from observed evidence and state limitations where they matter. If a later review shows that an earlier result is weak, I would rather rebuild it than keep a polished result I cannot defend.
